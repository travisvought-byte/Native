National Human Trafficking Hotline

Open to anyone

This hotline connects people who are in that situation, or who got out of it, with support and services to get help and stay safe. It is confidential and open 24 hours a day. Health care

Clinics run by the Indian Health Service, tribes and urban Indian programs, and how health insurance rules work for Native people.

Before you call: what to have ready and what to ask

Have ready

  • Tribal enrollment card or other proof of tribal affiliation, if you have one
  • Any insurance, Medicaid or Medicare card
  • A list of your medicines

Ask

  • Can I be seen here, and what proof do you need?
  • Do you offer the service I need, or where do you send people?
  • Will anything cost me money?

Indian Health Service, tribal and urban Indian clinics

Usually needs tribal enrollment

The Indian Health Service (IHS), tribal health programs and urban Indian programs give health care to American Indians and Alaska Natives. The IHS says it is not health insurance, and not an entitlement like Medicare or Medicaid.

Who it is for
The most common rule is enrollment in a federally recognized tribe. The exact policy is in the Indian Health Manual, so ask the clinic. Some tribally run clinics see only their own members.
How to start
Find the nearest clinic with the IHS Find Health Care tool, which lists IHS, tribal and urban clinics, and call ahead to confirm it offers what you need. To register, go to the clinic's patient registration office with proof of tribal enrollment.

IHS Find Health Care

Urban Indian health programs

For Native people

Nonprofit health programs in cities, governed by boards that are at least 51 percent American Indian or Alaska Native. The IHS funds 41 of them, with more than 80 sites in 22 states. Services can include primary care, traditional healing, mental health and substance use counseling, and youth, elder and job programs.

Purchased/Referred Care: when the IHS pays for care somewhere else

Usually needs tribal enrollment

Care at an IHS or tribal clinic is called direct care. Purchased/Referred Care (PRC) covers medical or dental care away from those clinics. The IHS says PRC is not an entitlement, and a referral does not mean the bill will be paid.

Who it is for
You must meet rules on tribal affiliation, where you live, giving notice, medical priority and other coverage you could use. PRC generally needs you to live on or near a reservation. It lasts 180 days after you move away; after that only direct care continues.
How to start
Before you get care outside the clinic, ask the clinic's PRC office what it needs from you and by when.

IHS Purchased/Referred Care

Health insurance rules for Native people (Marketplace, Medicaid, CHIP)

Rules differ: ask

Members of federally recognized tribes and Alaska Native (ANCSA) shareholders can sign up for a Marketplace health plan in any month and change plans up to once a month. With income between 100% and 300% of the federal poverty level they can get a plan with zero cost sharing, and they pay nothing for care from Indian health providers at any income. In Medicaid and CHIP, people eligible for IHS services pay no premiums and no out-of-pocket costs for care from IHS, tribal or urban Indian programs. You can keep using those clinics after you enroll.

Who it is for
Marketplace rules: members of federally recognized tribes and ANCSA shareholders. Medicaid and CHIP rules: people eligible for IHS services; Medicaid's definition is broader and includes descendants.
How to start
Apply at HealthCare.gov or call 1-800-318-2596, open 24 hours a day except holidays. Answer yes when asked whether anyone is American Indian or Alaska Native. Have a tribal enrollment card, a BIA certificate of Indian blood or other proof of tribal affiliation ready.

Call 1-800-318-2596 HealthCare.gov: American Indians and Alaska Natives

1-800-318-2596: HealthCare.gov call center. TTY 1-855-889-4325.

The Indian Child Welfare Act (ICWA): your family's rights

Rules differ: ask

ICWA is a federal law for state court cases about foster care, adoption or ending parental rights when the child is a tribal member, or is eligible for membership and has a parent who is a member. The state must make active efforts to keep the family together, tell the child's tribe and parents about the case, and follow placement preferences that favor family and community. The tribe can join the case, and a parent or the tribe can ask to move it to tribal court. ICWA does not cover custody disputes between parents.

Who it is for
Families of children who are tribal members or eligible for membership. Each tribe sets its own membership rules.
How to start
If you think these rights are being ignored, NICWA says to contact a lawyer (with Indian and family law experience if you can find one), legal services, or the child's tribe right away.

No VA copays for Native veterans

Rules differ: ask

Eligible American Indian and Alaska Native veterans do not pay VA copays for health care or urgent care. Copays still apply to domiciliary care, institutional respite care, institutional geriatric evaluation and nursing home care.

Who it is for
American Indian and Alaska Native veterans who send the VA their tribal documentation.
How to start
Send VA Form 10-334 with a copy of your official tribal documentation. For questions, call 1-800-698-2411.

Call 1-800-698-2411 VA: copay exemption
