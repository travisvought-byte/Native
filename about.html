About this site

Nina's Map is a free guide for Native people looking for help, for their Nation, or for community.

Dedicated to Nina, and hers to share.

Who is behind it

The site was built and is maintained by Travis Vought, an individual. It is not a tribal government or a government agency, and it does not speak for any Nation. It gathers in one place what Nations, agencies and Native organizations publish about themselves, in plain language.

It needs Native people to look it over. If you are a tribal citizen or descendant, or you work for a Nation, a tribal program or a Native organization, your corrections and your judgment about what belongs here are wanted: travis@vethomeguard.org.

What it covers so far

This first version covers the United States: national help by need, the government office of every federally recognized Nation, and a page for every state. Ohio and Michigan also list community groups and state programs. More states are being filled in. Help for First Nations, Inuit and Métis people in Canada and for Indigenous peoples in Mexico comes next.

Not here yet: Nations recognized only by a state, Nations still seeking recognition, and most local organizations outside Ohio and Michigan.

Where the listings come from

  • Nations: the Bureau of Indian Affairs Tribal Leaders Directory, retrieved November 17, 2026. We keep the name, town, state, phone number, website and map position of each government office. We leave out the names of elected leaders, which change with every election. The Bureau says the directory is not the official list of federally recognized tribes and may contain errors.
  • Programs and organizations: each one's own website, or a page from a government agency that lists it. Every listing shows the date it was checked and links to the page it came from.
  • Map shapes: U.S. Census Bureau state outlines.

Listings are read from published sources. When something is wrong or out of date, tell us and it will be fixed.

What the labels mean

Programs differ on who they serve. Some are open to anyone, some serve descendants, and some ask for proof of enrollment in a federally recognized tribe. Each listing carries one of these labels, taken from what the program itself says:

Open to anyone
No Native status or tribal card is asked for.
For Native people
Serves Native people. The program does not state that a tribal enrollment card is required, but ask.
Members and descendants
Serves enrolled tribal members and people descended from an enrolled member.
Usually needs tribal enrollment
Usually asks for proof that you are enrolled in a federally recognized tribe.
Rules differ: ask
Each tribe, office or program sets its own rule about who it serves.

For Nations and organizations

You decide how you are described. Names appear as the Bureau of Indian Affairs or the organization itself writes them. If you want a name, a description or a contact changed, or a listing removed, write to travis@vethomeguard.org.

This site does not publish ceremonial knowledge, the locations of sacred places, or anything else a Nation has not chosen to make public.

Your privacy and safety

There are no accounts and no forms, and the site stores nothing about you. One counter (GoatCounter) counts page views without cookies. Phone and text links open your own phone's apps.

The "Leave this site now" bar at the top of every page takes you to a weather page. It cannot clear your browser history. If someone checks your phone or computer, use a private browsing window, or a device they cannot get to.

Limits

This is general information, not legal, medical or financial advice. It does not decide who qualifies for a program; the program does. Phone numbers and rules change, so confirm details with the program before you rely on them.
