Support Native-led groups

If you want to help Native communities, the surest way is to ask Native-led groups what they need and give that. This page gathers what groups on Nina's Map are asking for, and how to give in ways that help.

What groups are asking for now

No group has posted an ask yet. As Nations and Native-led groups set up their free pages, what they ask for will appear here, in their own words, with a link to their page. Until then, the advice below is a good place to start.

Run a Native-led group? Get a free page and post what would help.

Giving money

  • Only a small share of philanthropy goes to Native communities, and Native-led organizations receive only part of that share, research by the Bridgespan Group with Native Americans in Philanthropy finds. Giving directly to groups led by Native people is one way to change that.
  • Before you give, the Federal Trade Commission suggests searching the group's name with words like "complaint", "review" or "scam", and using the IRS Tax Exempt Organization Search to see whether a gift is tax deductible.
  • Pay by credit card or check. The FTC warns against anyone who asks for donations in cash, by gift card or by wire transfer, and against anyone who rushes you.

Giving things

Ask first. After disasters, FEMA advises that money usually helps most, and that unrequested goods can pull volunteers away from helping people because they have to be sorted, moved and stored. Confirm with the group exactly what it needs before you collect or ship anything. When a group on this site posts a supplies list, it says what is needed and until when.

Buying from Native artists and businesses

  • Under federal law, work made after 1935 can be sold as "Indian", "Native American" or as the work of a particular Tribe only if the maker is a member of a federally or state-recognized Tribe, or is certified as an Indian artisan by their Tribe. The FTC says to be careful with words like "descent", "heritage" or "ancestry" next to a Tribe's name.
  • Get written proof of any claim about who made a piece, and a receipt that lists the maker and their Tribe.
  • The Indian Arts and Crafts Board keeps a Source Directory of arts and crafts businesses.

Giving time and attention

Go to the events groups invite the public to. Share their pages and their news. Offer the skills a group asks for, on its terms.
