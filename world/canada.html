Canada: help and community for First Nations, Inuit and Métis

If you are First Nations, Inuit or Métis, this page brings together help made for you: Hope for Wellness for any Indigenous person in Canada, Non-Insured Health Benefits, Jordan's Principle and the Inuit Child First Initiative for children, and Friendship Centres if you live in a city or town. Every First Nation and Inuit community is listed further down.

Canada recognizes three groups of Indigenous peoples in its Constitution: First Nations, Inuit and Métis. The map shows the administrative office of every First Nation registered with Indigenous Services Canada and every Inuit community, from the Government of Canada's open data. Each First Nation links to its official profile, which has its address and phone.

Sources: Indigenous Services Canada: First Nations Location (open data) · Inuit Communities Location (open data).

Need help right now?

  • Hope for Wellness, for all Indigenous people: call 1-855-242-3310.
  • Suicide or any crisis: call 988 or text 988.
  • In immediate danger, call 911.

Crisis and safety

Hope for Wellness Help Line

For Indigenous people

Counselling and crisis help by phone or online chat, 24 hours a day, for Indigenous people anywhere in Canada.

Who it is for
All Indigenous people across Canada.
How to start
Call 1-855-242-3310, or open the chat on the website.

Call 1-855-242-3310 hopeforwellness.ca (chat)

1-855-242-3310: Any time.

Checked November 20, 2026. Source

9-8-8 Suicide Crisis Helpline

Open to anyone

Call or text 988 any time if you are thinking about suicide or worried about someone. Free, in every province and territory.

Who it is for
Anyone in Canada.
How to start
Call or text 988. In an emergency, call 911.

Call 988 Text 988 988.ca

Checked November 20, 2026. Source

National Indian Residential School Crisis Line

Rules differ: ask

Crisis support, 24 hours a day, for former residential school students and their families, with referrals to other health supports.

Who it is for
Former Indian Residential School students and their families.
How to start
Call 1-866-925-4419, any time.

Call 1-866-925-4419 Resolution Health Support Program

Checked November 20, 2026. Source

Health care

Non-Insured Health Benefits (NIHB)

Rules differ: ask

Federal coverage for health costs that provincial plans and private insurance do not cover, such as prescription drugs, dental and vision care, medical supplies, mental health counselling and medical travel.

Who it is for
Residents of Canada who are registered First Nations or recognized Inuit. First Nations in British Columbia are covered through the First Nations Health Authority instead, and Inuit in Nunavik through their regional board.
How to start
Read who is eligible, then ask your health provider or pharmacist to bill NIHB.

Who is eligible

Checked November 20, 2026. Source

Children, youth and family

Jordan's Principle

Rules differ: ask

Funding so First Nations children can get the health, social and education products, services and supports they need, on or off reserve. Named for Jordan River Anderson of Norway House Cree Nation.

Who it is for
First Nations children in Canada.
How to start
Call the Jordan's Principle Call Centre, open 24 hours. It is not a crisis line; if a child needs immediate care, call 911.

Call 1-855-572-4453 Jordan's Principle

1-855-572-4453: 1-855-JP-CHILD, any time. Teletypewriter: 1-866-553-0554.

Checked November 20, 2026. Source

Inuit Child First Initiative

Rules differ: ask

Funding so Inuit children have equal access to health, social and education products, services and supports. Free to apply.

Who it is for
Inuit children, wherever they live in Canada.
How to start
Contact your regional focal point, or call the national call centre, open 24 hours.

Call 1-855-572-4453 Supporting Inuit children

More: Inuit Tapiriit Kanatami: how to apply in each region

Checked November 20, 2026. Source

Community and culture

Friendship Centres

For Indigenous people

Community hubs for Indigenous people living in cities and towns, with programs in health, shelter, youth, justice and more. Over 100 centres belong to the National Association of Friendship Centres.

Who it is for
Indigenous people living in urban areas; many programs are open to everyone.
How to start
Find the centre nearest you on the association's map.
