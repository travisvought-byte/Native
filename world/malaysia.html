Malaysia: help and community for Orang Asal

Indigenous peoples, together called Orang Asal, are about 11% of Malaysia's 32.4 million people (2020 census). On the Peninsula they are known as Orang Asli and number about 227,900 (2025); most Orang Asal live in Sabah and Sarawak on Borneo.

Sources: The Indigenous World 2026 (IWGIA): Malaysia.

Background: read the Malaysia chapter of The Indigenous World 2026, the yearly report of the International Work Group for Indigenous Affairs (IWGIA), on land, rights, health and recent events.

Crisis and safety

Talian HEAL 15555 (mental health crisis line)

Open to anyone

The national mental health crisis line, run by counselling officers of the Ministry of Health (KKM). It gives emotional support and early help in a crisis, including thoughts of suicide, depression, anxiety, panic and stress.

Who it is for
Anyone in Malaysia. Help is given in English, Malay and Tamil.
How to start
Call 15555, any day from 8 am to midnight, public holidays included. In immediate danger, call 999.

Peoples, Nations and their governments

JAKOA, Department of Orang Asli Development (Jabatan Kemajuan Orang Asli)

For Indigenous people Putrajaya

The federal department responsible for Orang Asli development on Peninsular Malaysia, including education, economic and community programmes in Orang Asli villages.

Who it is for
Orang Asli people and villages on Peninsular Malaysia.
How to start
Call the head office in Putrajaya, or contact your state JAKOA office, and ask which programme fits your need.
Address
Aras 10, Blok A, Bangunan Suasana PjH, Presint 2, Putrajaya

Call 03-8090 3600 jakoa.gov.my: contact
