Finding community

Places and groups where Native people gather: community centers, youth councils, recovery circles and tribal colleges.

Community groups are listed so far for Ohio and Michigan. Urban Indian centers in 21 states and 33 tribal colleges in 13 states are further down, and every state page lists its Nations, clinics and tribal lands.

Community centers and groups, by state

Michigan

Native American Outreach Services (Michigan Department of Health and Human Services)

Members and descendants

State outreach workers who help Native families find services that fit their culture, get through state and federal programs including child welfare cases, and identify their heritage and tribal enrollment.

Who it is for
Households that include a tribal member, citizen or descendant. Workers can also help if you are not sure of your tribal affiliation. The state lists no income rule.
How to start
Find the outreach worker for your county on the state's contact map and ask to start services. The state uses a referral form, DHS-382.

American Indian Health and Family Services

For Native people Detroit

A nonprofit health and community wellness center in southwest Detroit, and one of the urban Indian health programs. It serves seven counties: Livingston, Macomb, Monroe, Oakland, St. Clair, Washtenaw and Wayne.

Who it is for
Native people in southeastern Michigan. The center also serves other people in the area.
How to start
Call (313) 846-3718, or go to 4880 Lawndale Street, Detroit.
Address

North American Indian Association of Detroit

For Native people Detroit area

Founded in 1940 as the North American Indian Club. It offers education assistance, employment and training help, and cultural and social events, and the U.S. Department of Labor lists it as a Native job training grantee.

Who it is for
Native people in the Detroit area.

Ohio

Native American Indian Center of Central Ohio (NAICCO)

For Native people Columbus

An intertribal community gathering place in Columbus for Native people in Ohio, including people who live far from their ancestral homelands. It works on cultural preservation, community development and wellness, and describes itself as an urban Indian center.

Who it is for
Native people and families in Ohio.
How to start
Email naicco1975@gmail.com, or follow NAICCO on Facebook, Instagram or YouTube to hear about gatherings.
Address
67 East Innis Avenue, Columbus, Ohio. Mail: P.O. Box 7705, Columbus, OH 43207

Email naicco1975@gmail.com naicco.com

Urban Native Collective

For Native people Cincinnati

A cultural home and gathering place for American Indian, Alaska Native and Indigenous peoples in the Greater Cincinnati region, serving Ohio, Kentucky and Indiana. Its Native Sovereignty Center has a lending library and hosts events. The collective also runs an urban garden and a program that helps Indigenous women, women of color, and Two-Spirit and non-binary relatives get mental health care.

Who it is for
Native and Indigenous people in the Cincinnati region.
How to start
Visit the Native Sovereignty Center at 1710 Blue Rock Street in Northside, Monday to Friday, 10 a.m. to 4 p.m., or email info@urbannativecollective.org.
Address
1710 Blue Rock Street, Cincinnati, OH 45223

Email info@urbannativecollective.org urbannativecollective.org

American Indian Studies at Ohio State University

Open to anyone Columbus

A program at Ohio State that works with the Newark Earthworks Center and with Native student groups on campus. It says all of its programming is free and open to the public, and its website lists campus events and courses. The university also has a staff member for American Indian and Indigenous student services.

Who it is for
Anyone for public events. Students at Ohio State for student services and student groups.
How to start
Check the program's website for upcoming events. If you are a student, ask the program how to reach American Indian and Indigenous student services.

From anywhere

Urban Indian health programs

For Native people

Nonprofit health programs in cities, governed by boards that are at least 51 percent American Indian or Alaska Native. The IHS funds 41 of them, with more than 80 sites in 22 states. Services can include primary care, traditional healing, mental health and substance use counseling, and youth, elder and job programs.

Who it is for
Native people living in the city the program serves. Ask the program how it decides who it can see.
How to start
Find a program on the IHS map of Urban Indian Organizations, then call it.

Wellbriety circles (White Bison)

For Native people

White Bison is a Native American-operated nonprofit behind the Wellbriety Movement: sobriety, recovery and wellness grounded in Native culture. It keeps a list of Wellbriety circles, which are recovery and talking circles that meet in person and on Zoom.

Who it is for
Each circle says whether it is open to new people. Many are.
How to start
Open the circle list, find one near you or one on Zoom, and contact the facilitator listed for that circle.

UNITY: United National Indian Tribal Youth

Members and descendants

A national network of Native youth councils that builds leadership through trainings and conferences. Councils can be tribal, village, urban, school or college groups.

Who it is for
American Indian, Alaska Native and Native Hawaiian youth, including descendants. Suggested ages 14 to 24.
How to start
Join through a youth council, or as an individual member for $50 a year. Call 480-718-9793 with questions.

Your own Nation

If you know which Nation you come from, call its government office and ask what gatherings, classes or events are coming up, and whether descendants are welcome. The Nations directory has the numbers. If you are not sure which Nation, start with Finding your Nation.
