Native New Mexico: tribal Nations, urban Indian health and Indian health care

New Mexico is home to the governments of Nations such as the Jicarilla Apache Nation, the Mescalero Apache Tribe and the Zuni Tribe. If your Nation is here, its office is on this page with a way to reach it. Also on this page: urban Indian programs in Albuquerque, which often serve any Native person; Indian health hospitals in Crownpoint, Gallup and Shiprock; and tribal colleges such as Navajo Technical University and Southwestern Indian Polytechnic Institute.

Need help right now?

  • Thinking about suicide, or in any crisis: call 988 or text 988, any time. Veterans: dial 988, then press 1.
  • Hurt or threatened by a partner or family member, or sexually assaulted: call or text the StrongHearts Native Helpline, 1-844-762-8483.
  • Being made to work or sell sex: call 1-888-373-7888 or text 233733.
  • In immediate danger, call 911.

Native community and help in New Mexico

Health care

First Nations Community HealthSource

For Native people Albuquerque

An Urban Indian Organization funded by the Indian Health Service. The Indian Health Service lists it as offering a medical clinic with a full range of outpatient care.

Who it is for
American Indian and Alaska Native people living in or near the city. Ask what proof of tribal affiliation or descent it needs.
How to start
Call and ask how to become a patient or client, and which services are offered at the site nearest you.

Call 505-262-2481 Website

Indian health clinics and hospitals in New Mexico

47 places listed by the Indian Health Service. Clinics run by the Indian Health Service or a tribe mostly serve members and descendants of federally recognized tribes; some serve everyone in the area. Urban Indian programs often serve any Native person. Call first and ask whether you can be seen and what to bring.
