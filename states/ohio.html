Native Ohio: community, help and the Nations tied to Ohio

If you are Native and living in Ohio, start with the community that is already here: Native centers and gathering places in Columbus, Cincinnati and Cleveland, and Ohio's Native job training program in Tallmadge, near Akron. If your Nation's homelands include Ohio, its office is listed below with a way to reach it.

No federally recognized Nation has its government in Ohio today. The Ohio History Connection works with 45 federally recognized Nations that are tied to Ohio through homelands, historic sites and treaties. Many were forced out in the 1800s under federal removal policy, and their governments are now in Indiana, Kansas, Michigan, Minnesota, Missouri, Montana, New York, Oklahoma, Texas and Wisconsin. Native people from many Nations live in Ohio now, and the community groups below are where they gather.

Sources: Ohio History Connection: American Indian Relations · Ohio History Connection: a reflection on Native influences on Ohio. Checked November 17, 2026.

Need help right now?

  • Thinking about suicide, or in any crisis: call 988 or text 988, any time. Veterans: dial 988, then press 1.
  • Hurt or threatened by a partner or family member, or sexually assaulted: call or text the StrongHearts Native Helpline, 1-844-762-8483.
  • Being made to work or sell sex: call 1-888-373-7888 or text 233733.
  • In immediate danger, call 911.

Children, youth and family

Ohio's rules under the Indian Child Welfare Act

Rules differ: ask

Ohio's children services rules require county and private agencies to follow the Indian Child Welfare Act. The act applies when a child is an Indian child and is in a custody case, including involuntary cases and emergency removals. An agency may not weigh a family's part in tribal life, or the child's blood quantum, when deciding whether the act applies.

Who it is for
Families with a child who is a member of a federally recognized tribe, or who may be eligible for membership, and who are dealing with an Ohio children services agency or court.
How to start
Tell the caseworker and the court, in writing if you can, that the child is or may be an Indian child, and name the tribe or tribes. The rules say an agency has reason to know once anyone in the case tells it so, or a parent or the child has a tribal membership card. Then call the tribe.

School and scholarships

Myaamia Heritage Award Program (Miami University)

Usually needs tribal enrollment Oxford

A four-year undergraduate program at Miami University for citizens of the Miami Tribe of Oklahoma. It waives instructional fees for up to eight semesters and includes a weekly Heritage class on Myaamia history, language and culture. Course fees are not covered.

Who it is for
Enrolled citizens of the Miami Tribe of Oklahoma who are admitted to Miami University and enroll at the Oxford campus.
How to start
Apply to Miami University the same way as any other student, and call the Miami Tribe Relations office to ask about the award.
Address
Myaamia Center, 200 Bonham House, Oxford, OH 45056

Call 513-529-5648 Website

513-529-5648: Miami Tribe Relations office at the Myaamia Center.

Community and culture

Native American Indian Center of Central Ohio (NAICCO)

For Native people Columbus

An intertribal community gathering place in Columbus for Native people in Ohio, including people who live far from their ancestral homelands. It works on cultural preservation, community development and wellness, and describes itself as an urban Indian center.

Who it is for
Native people and families in Ohio.
How to start
Email naicco1975@gmail.com, or follow NAICCO on Facebook, Instagram or YouTube to hear about gatherings.
Address
67 East Innis Avenue, Columbus, Ohio. Mail: P.O. Box 7705, Columbus, OH 43207

Email naicco1975@gmail.com naicco.com

Checked November 17, 2026. Source

Urban Native Collective

For Native people Cincinnati

A cultural home and gathering place for American Indian, Alaska Native and Indigenous peoples in the Greater Cincinnati region, serving Ohio, Kentucky and Indiana. Its Native Sovereignty Center has a lending library and hosts events. The collective also runs an urban garden and a program that helps Indigenous women, women of color, and Two-Spirit and non-binary relatives get mental health care.

Who it is for
Native and Indigenous people in the Cincinnati region.
How to start
Visit the Native Sovereignty Center at 1710 Blue Rock Street in Northside, Monday to Friday, 10 a.m. to 4 p.m., or email info@urbannativecollective.org.
Address
1710 Blue Rock Street, Cincinnati, OH 45223

Email info@urbannativecollective.org urbannativecollective.org

American Indian Studies at Ohio State University

Open to anyone Columbus

A program at Ohio State that works with the Newark Earthworks Center and with Native student groups on campus. It says all of its programming is free and open to the public, and its website lists campus events and courses. The university also has a staff member for American Indian and Indigenous student services.

Who it is for
Anyone for public events. Students at Ohio State for student services and student groups.
How to start
Check the program's website for upcoming events. If you are a student, ask the program how to reach American Indian and Indigenous student services.
