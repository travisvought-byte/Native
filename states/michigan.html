Native Michigan: community, tribal Nations and Indian health care

Michigan is home to the governments of Nations such as the Hannahville Indian Community, the Bay Mills Indian Community and the Pokagon Band of Potawatomi Indians. If your Nation is here, its office is on this page with a way to reach it. Also on this page: urban Indian programs in Detroit, which often serve any Native person; the Native American Outreach Services (Michigan Department of Health and Human Services); and American Indian Health and Family Services in Detroit.

Twelve federally recognized Nations have their governments in Michigan. Michigan also has its own law, the Michigan Indian Family Preservation Act of 2013, which strengthens the Indian Child Welfare Act in Michigan courts.

Sources: Bureau of Indian Affairs Tribal Leaders Directory · Michigan Department of Health and Human Services policy on the Michigan Indian Family Preservation Act. Checked November 17, 2026.

Need help right now?

  • Thinking about suicide, or in any crisis: call 988 or text 988, any time. Veterans: dial 988, then press 1.
  • Hurt or threatened by a partner or family member, or sexually assaulted: call or text the StrongHearts Native Helpline, 1-844-762-8483.
  • Being made to work or sell sex: call 1-888-373-7888 or text 233733.
  • In immediate danger, call 911.

Children, youth and family

Native American Outreach Services (Michigan Department of Health and Human Services)

Members and descendants

State outreach workers who help Native families find services that fit their culture, get through state and federal programs including child welfare cases, and identify their heritage and tribal enrollment.

Who it is for
Households that include a tribal member, citizen or descendant. Workers can also help if you are not sure of your tribal affiliation. The state lists no income rule.
How to start
Find the outreach worker for your county on the state's contact map and ask to start services. The state uses a referral form, DHS-382.

School and scholarships

Michigan Indian Tuition Waiver

Usually needs tribal enrollment

Waives tuition at Michigan's public community colleges and universities. It covers tuition only. Full-time or part-time status and your course of study do not limit it.

Who it is for
You must qualify for admission, be at least one-quarter Native American by blood quantum and an enrolled citizen of a U.S. federally recognized tribe (your tribal enrollment department certifies both), and have lived in Michigan for at least 12 months in a row. The state says citizens of state or historic tribes and of Canadian First Nations are not eligible.
How to start
Apply online, by phone at 888-447-2687 (Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 4:45 p.m.), or on paper, with a photocopy of your tribal ID. Your tribe certifies the application and may send it to the state for you.

Call 888-447-2687 Email mitw@michigan.gov Michigan Indian Tuition Waiver

Legal help and rights

Michigan Indian Legal Services

Rules differ: ask Traverse City

A nonprofit law office that gives legal help, advice and representation in U.S. and tribal courts: protecting families and cultural practices, basic needs and rights, property, and safety from abuse and exploitation. It is free for people whose income qualifies.

Who it is for
People with low income. The office focuses on tribal and federal Indian law problems.
How to start
Call 231-947-0122. Its page on Michigan Legal Help lists phone hours of weekdays 9 a.m. to noon and 1 to 3 p.m.

814 S. Garfield Ave., Suite A, Traverse City, MI 49686
