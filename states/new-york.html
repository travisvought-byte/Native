Native New York: tribal Nations, urban Indian health and Indian health care

New York is home to the governments of Nations such as the Tonawanda Band of Seneca, the Onondaga Nation and the Tuscarora Nation. If your Nation is here, its office is on this page with a way to reach it. Also on this page: urban Indian programs in Long Island City, which often serve any Native person; tribal and Indian Health Service clinics; and tribal lands such as the Oneida Nation Reservation and Allegany Reservation.

Need help right now?

  • Thinking about suicide, or in any crisis: call 988 or text 988, any time. Veterans: dial 988, then press 1.
  • Hurt or threatened by a partner or family member, or sexually assaulted: call or text the StrongHearts Native Helpline, 1-844-762-8483.
  • Being made to work or sell sex: call 1-888-373-7888 or text 233733.
  • In immediate danger, call 911.

Native community and help in New York

Health care

New York Indian Council, Inc.

For Native people Long Island City

An Urban Indian Organization funded by the Indian Health Service. The Indian Health Service lists it as offering help finding and getting to health care (outreach and referral).

Who it is for
American Indian and Alaska Native people living in or near the city. Ask what proof of tribal affiliation or descent it needs.
How to start
Call and ask how to become a patient or client, and which services are offered at the site nearest you.

Call 718-215-8417 Indian Health Service listing

Indian health clinics and hospitals in New York

10 places listed by the Indian Health Service. Clinics run by the Indian Health Service or a tribe mostly serve members and descendants of federally recognized tribes; some serve everyone in the area. Urban Indian programs often serve any Native person. Call first and ask whether you can be seen and what to bring.
